How Dry-Hire Works

We don't profit on alcohol. That's the whole model.

Dry-hire means we bring the bar, the bartenders and the experience, and the alcohol is bought at cost for the event. Ohio's liquor licensing is built around permanent establishments, so there's no event license a mobile bar can buy. Rather than work around that, we built the business on it. We don't sell alcohol and we never mark it up.

It doesn't matter to us who buys it. You, your client, or us on their behalf. What matters is that it's bought at retail, which is where the savings live. A bottle that runs $22 at Costco can carry a $60+ open-bar charge anywhere else. On most events that gap is larger than our entire fee.

Don't want to make the run? That's our job too. We build the list, do the shopping and hand over every receipt.

We plan generously on anything that can go back, so the bar never runs dry. Then we return every unopened bottle ourselves and the refund goes to whoever paid. No guessing, no leftover cases in a closet, no return trip for you or your client.

Procurement is a flat service fee, and it shows up as one line on your quote. You'll know what it is before you commit to anything.

$0

Markup on alcohol. Ever. We don't sell it, so there's nothing to mark up. Whoever buys pays retail and we bring everything else the bar needs.

Meet the Owner

Born and raised in Cincinnati, Ohio (and before you ask, Madeira High School). I created 513Sips for two reasons: to pay homage to the city I love and to build something around a passion I have always had, bringing people together and finding ways to serve others.

To my friends, I'm known as “The Hub,” the one who hosts, makes the plans and finds a reason to get everyone together. As president of the Greater Cincinnati Penn State Alumni Association, I play a similar role on a larger scale. Since 2020, I've organized dozens of philanthropy events to give back to the community we live in.

Along the way, I started to notice something about the best gatherings. A good bar is about more than what is being poured. It becomes a gathering place. Stories get told there. Friends linger there. Sometimes the moments you remember most happen there.

During one philanthropy event, we were sponsored by a beer company and I used this as an opportunity to expand on a long-standing passion of mine.

So I built a bar.

513Sips grew from equal parts friends and family, a love of bringing people together, a heavy pour of serving others and a dash of wanting to make every event feel a little more special.

By day, I'm a managing business consultant. That world has taught me how to build processes, create the right tools and run an operation that people can depend on. What I didn't expect was how much I would love the creative side of this business:

  • Making syrups from scratch.
  • Candying citrus and fruit by hand.
  • Finding a new spin on a familiar cocktail.
  • Working with a client to carry a theme from the menu to the garnish to the bar itself.

Little details I now obsess over for every event, because they are the details that make things feel special.

I know what it feels like to sit on both sides of an event. I've been the organizer putting my name behind it and the vendor being trusted to deliver it. That's why I stay personally involved from the first conversation and menu planning through setup and the final pour.

Eddie Gady
Owner, 513Sips
